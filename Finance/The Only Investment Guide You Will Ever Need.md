## Tax Strategies
- Roth IRA - You can contribute up to $6k/Year into a Roth IRA if you adjusted Gross Income is less than $140k a year.
	- If married and file jointly, you can contribute $6k/Year if less than $200k AGI.
	- If older than 50, can contribute an extra $1k.
- Traditional IRA gives you a tax break on the way in, and contribution is deductable.
- Roth IRA gives you a tax break on the way out, every penny you withdraw is not taxed, and there is no need to withdraw the money.
- ##### Choose Roth IRA if you are in a low tax bracket now and expect to be in a higher tax bracket when you retire. Choose Traditional IRA if you are in a higher tax bracket now and expect to be in a lower one when you retire.

## Annuities 
- An **annuity** is a financial product—usually sold by insurance companies—that’s designed to turn a lump sum (or a series of payments) into **regular income**, often for **retirement**.
- Recommended to not buy annuities:
	- Income is fully taxed as you withdraw it.
	- Funds are locked up to 59.5 years old.
	- 10% early withdraw fee.
	- Over a long time, stocks are almost sure to beat fixed rate investments.
	- Any gains in dividends that you withdraw are fully taxed as income.
	- No initial tax deduction.

## Stock Market Notes
- Growing companies pay little to nothing in taxable dividends.
- Tax on gains is deferred until you choose to take them your profit.
- Long term capital gains and qualifying dividends are taxed less heavily than ordinary income. 
- 3 Main Reasons to invest in stocks:
	1. Stocks offer potential to keep up with inflation.
	2. Over (very) long run, stocks outperform safer investments because market rewards risk.
	3. Stocks can act as a tax shelter, if all goes well. Long term gains are taxed less heavily than ordinary income. No taxes on gains until you choose to take them!
- Most people should do stock investing through no load index funds.
- Bullish means people think stock market will rise.
- Bearish means people think stock market will fall.
- Most sensible way for most people to invest in stocks:
	1. Only invest money that you wont have to touch for years. If you don't have money like that, don't buy stocks.
	2. Buy low, sell high. Opportunities are best when markets look bad.
- The best time to get in the stock market is when the economy is sinking fast, and stocks faster. But do not want to try and "time the market".
- Avoid enthusiasm when the market is judged to be healthy. Think: people are talking about it and are expecting good news. If the good news comes, it probably won't impact the market too much, BUT, if bad news comes, it will impact the market negatively by quite a lot. ASK: What types of news do you expect vs what is generally expected?
- Diversify over time by not investing all at once and avoiding peaks and valleys.
- ### DCA:
	- Dollar cost averaging is investing in a stock or a market in equal dollar amounts on a regular basis. Ex: $3k invested regularly at the end of the year.
	- DCA forces you to buy more when a stock is lower, which can allow you to make money over time as a stock fluctuates. But, could make you lose a lot if stock just continues to go down.
- Diversify over several stocks in several industries!
- Beware of the deceptive P/E Ratio... This is the guide that most investors use to get a quick fix of a stock. It tells you how much you have to pay (P) for a dollar of earnings (E).
- When choosing a stock you have to guess how well a company will do, whether that will make the stock go up or down, and which way the market is moving.
- Occasionally, companies will have 2 classes of common stock: A and B. The only difference between the 2 are voting rights, but otherwise treated the same. Profits are the same, dividends are the same, etc. If company is bought by a larger company, both classes of stock get it. Usually both trade for about the same price.
- Blockbuster stock example shows stock market inefficiencies.
